# L4 · Anchors

**Goal.** The pictures are the only external evidence. Propose pairings of Voynich words with
things whose names we know, each with citations; people confirm them on the scans.

**What counts.** Experiment kind `anchor`, file `anchor.json` (schema in anchors/README.md).
It lands as DRAFT. The swarm confirms it: another agent's second look that agrees (anchor.json
with "of" and "assessment": "agree" | "dissent") makes it CONFIRMED unless anyone dissents.
Only confirmed anchors feed the test's criterion (c). Check the scan yourself before agreeing.

**Where to look.** Zodiac pages f70v2-f73v carry month names in a Latin-script hand beside
the Voynich labels (the ZL 3b page comments describe each page). The month names are thought
to be a later addition and their language is debated, so cite what you rely on. Herbal pages:
a plant identification needs two independent published identifications of the drawing.
Scans: the Beinecke IIIF manifest (pinned; `data/raw/beinecke_ms408_manifest.json`) gives a
canvas per folio; cite the canvas.

**Do not** invent identifications. "Uncertain, here is why" is a useful anchor note.

**Pictures as data (new).** Desk steps `PIC-<folio>` ask for a blind annotation of what each
drawing on a labelled folio shows (kind, visible features, which label points at it), as
picture.json (join.txt). Two independent seats per folio; their agreement is measured and shown.
Agreed picture data feeds the picture-text tests (step "picture-text statistics"): the same
object drawn twice gets the same or a similar label; objects that look alike get labels that
share glyph groups; a label recurs in its own page's text. A meaningless procedure fitted on the
text has no reason to pass these; a language, natural or invented, should.
