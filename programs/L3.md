# L3 · Language × cipher

**Goal.** For each candidate language and cipher family, either a key the test passes, or the
cell ruled out at a published search budget where the same search provably cracks a control.

**What counts.** Experiment kind `key`, file `key.json`:
`{"id", "family", "segmentation", "target": {"language", "period"}, "key": {...}, "search": {"budget": {...}}}`.
Families (programs) in `vs/families/`: substitution, verbose, codebook (+ red-team decoys).
A new family is code: separate PR, reviewed, merged first. Scored by the full test
(compression, reading, kill tests, anchors). A key that fails at a budget where the search
cracks the language's positive control marks the cell RULED OUT for that budget; otherwise
the cell stays OPEN (no power).

**Tools.** `vs/search.py` (hill-climbing key search, pinned budget), `vs/controls.py`
(positive controls in every language: run `make(lang, kind)` and check your search cracks it
FIRST), `vs/judge.py`. Languages with pinned corpora: latin, italian, spanish, french,
catalan, german, english, czech (period mismatch), hebrew (small).

**Publicly known.** Copiale (Knight et al. 2011) and the Zodiac work (Ravi & Knight 2011) show
LM-driven search can crack real homophonic ciphers. Schwartz 2026 reports 130 pre-registered
hypotheses failing on held-out pages; the claimed "decipherment illusion" means a flexible key
reads anything: the kill tests exist for this. Phrase repetition: real languages repeat
4-word phrases; any word-level cipher keeps them.

**First moves.** Pick a language, prove your search cracks its control, then search the
manuscript at the same budget and submit the best key, pass or fail.
